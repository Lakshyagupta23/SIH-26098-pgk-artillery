Design Rationale: Thermal Reserve Battery vs Ram-Air Turbine

The Aegis-155 utilizes a Thermal Reserve Battery Pack instead of a traditional Ram-Air Turbine (RAT). At setback forces exceeding 15,000g, rotating mechanical components in a RAT are highly susceptible to bearing shear and structural failure. A thermal battery remains completely inert until firing, providing a 20+ year shelf life, zero moving parts, and guaranteed activation under extreme gun-launch shock via pyrotechnic squib.

Model Assumptions & Limitations

What the simulator proves

  • The mathematical viability of a 2-axis canard system to reduce trajectory dispersion.
  • The statistical impact of environmental and systemic uncertainties on overall CEP.
  • The theoretical reduction in CEP compared to an unguided 155mm shell.

What the simulator does NOT prove

  • High-G survivability of the physical electronic components (Requires sled track/gun-launch testing).
  • Actual real-world RF performance of the FMCW radar under high spin rates.
  • Final operational validation of the Aegis-155 system.
Domain Assumption / Implementation Details
Atmosphere Model ISA standard atmosphere model. Density varies logarithmically with altitude.
Drag Model Mach-dependent $C_d$ coefficient using a simplified piece-wise linear approximation for supersonic transitions.
Wind Model Vector wind components with optional discrete altitude shear bands.
Navigation Model Perfect state knowledge conceptually degraded by Gaussian noise in GNSS/INS modes.
Actuator Model Instantaneous force application decoupled from projectile spin dynamics.
Numerical Integration 4th-Order Runge-Kutta (RK4) integration with 0.04s timestep (25Hz).
Sensor Noise Modeled as white Gaussian noise across position components based on expected hardware specifications.
Control Model Proportional-Derivative (PD) trajectory correction mimicking 2-axis canard actuation against the cross-track error.
Fuze-Event Model Evaluates terminal state sequentially (Impact, PD/Time, Proximity) assuming perfect threshold detection with fixed response delays.
Simplifications Point-mass kinematics; no 6-DOF moments, pitch/yaw damping, or rigid body rotational dynamics computed.
Unsupported Physics Magnus effect, Coriolis force, Epicyclic swerve, and aerodynamic blanking of canards are excluded.
Known Limitations Control authority is approximated as a lateral acceleration rather than physical canard lift equations. Simulation may overstate corrective capability in terminal dive.
